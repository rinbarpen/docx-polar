# English Patent Writing Guide (USPTO / EPO / PCT)

This guide covers formatting and content conventions for patent applications filed with the USPTO (United States), EPO (European Patent Office), and under the PCT (Patent Cooperation Treaty).

---

## Jurisdiction Overview

| Aspect | USPTO | EPO | PCT |
|--------|-------|-----|-----|
| Filing language | English | English/French/German | English/French/German/Spanish/Chinese/etc. |
| Examination | Substantive | Substantive | Preliminary only |
| Claim format | Single sentence per claim | Two-part form (pre-characterizing/characterizing) | Varies by elected office |
| Abstract | 150 words max | 150 words recommended | 150-350 words |
| Drawings | Required if helpful | Required for most cases | Required if helpful |
| Design patent | Yes (ornamental) | Yes (Community Design) | Not directly |

---

## Utility Patent Structure (USPTO/EPO/PCT)

### Title
- All capitals
- Technical and descriptive, not marketing
- Usually 7-15 words

### Cross-Reference to Related Applications
- USPTO requirement: list any prior applications claiming priority
- Format: "This application claims priority to U.S. Provisional Application No. XX/XXX,XXX, filed [date]."

### Field of the Invention
- One or two sentences identifying the technical field
- Format: "The present invention relates to [field], and more particularly to [specific area]."

### Background of the Invention
- Describe prior art and its limitations
- Be objective — do not disparage competitors
- Cite relevant patents and publications
- 300-800 words

### Brief Summary of the Invention
- Describe what the invention is (not how it works in detail)
- Corresponds to independent claims
- End with technical advantages / effects
- 200-500 words

### Brief Description of the Drawings
- List each figure with one sentence
- Format: "FIG. 1 is a schematic diagram illustrating a system according to an embodiment of the present invention."
- Each figure gets one sentence

### Detailed Description of the Invention
- The longest and most important section
- Describe at least one embodiment in detail
- Reference figures by number ("as shown in FIG. 1")
- Include specific values, ranges, materials where applicable
- Must support all claims — every claimed feature needs description
- 1500-5000 words

### Claims
- The legal boundary of the patent
- Independent claims: broadest scope (1-3 for USPTO, 1 for EPO)
- Dependent claims: narrowing limitations
- USPTO: "What is claimed is:" preamble
- EPO: Two-part form "characterized in that"
- Each claim is a single sentence
- Hanging indent format (left 720, hanging 360 in DXA)

### Abstract
- Brief summary suitable for searching
- USPTO: 150 words maximum
- Include key technical features
- Title: "ABSTRACT OF THE DISCLOSURE"

## Claims Formatting

### Independent Claim (USPTO)

```
1. A method for [function], comprising:
   [step A];
   [step B];
   [step C].
```

### Dependent Claim (USPTO)

```
2. The method of claim 1, wherein [additional limitation].

3. The method of claim 1, further comprising [additional step].
```

### Independent Claim (EPO — Two-Part Form)

```
1. A method for [function] comprising [steps A, B, C],
   characterized in that [distinguishing feature].
```

### Multiple Dependent Claims

```
5. The method of claim 2 or 3, wherein [further limitation].
```

Note: A multiple dependent claim cannot itself serve as a basis for another multiple dependent claim (USPTO 35 USC 112).

## Design Patent Structure (USPTO)

US design patents protect the **ornamental design** of an article of manufacture. They do not protect functional features.

### Requirements
- Title in ALL CAPS: name of the article
- Single inventor (for USPTO — can include multiple inventors via assignment)
- No abstract needed
- No claims beyond a single sentence

### Figure Descriptions
List each figure in order. Format:

```
FIG. 1 is a perspective view of a [article] showing my new design.
FIG. 2 is a front elevational view thereof.
FIG. 3 is a rear elevational view thereof.
FIG. 4 is a left side elevational view thereof.
FIG. 5 is a right side elevational view thereof.
FIG. 6 is a top plan view thereof.
FIG. 7 is a bottom plan view thereof.
```

### Claim

```
CLAIM
The ornamental design for a [article], as shown and described.
```

### Drawings
- Must include all views (perspective + 6 orthographic)
- Shading required for curved surfaces
- Broken/dashed lines indicate unclaimed environment
- Solid lines indicate claimed design

### EPO Community Design
- Simpler process — no examination
- Requires representation of the design (images/views)
- Indication of product
- Optionally: description, classification

## Common USPTO Formatting Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Paper size | A4 or US Letter (8.5" x 11") |
| Font | Times New Roman, 12pt minimum |
| Line spacing | 1.5 or double-spaced |
| Margins | 1 inch (2.54 cm) all sides |
| Paragraph numbering | [0001], [0002], etc. |
| Claim numbering | 1., 2., 3., etc. — each claim a single sentence |
| Abstract word limit | 150 words |
| Figure margins | 1 inch minimum, preferably more |

## PCT-Specific Notes

- PCT application is a "placeholder" — national/regional phase required later
- Abstract: 50-150 words
- Claims: fees increase after 15 claims
- Sequence listings: separate file required for nucleotide/amino acid sequences
- International search report (ISR) issued by ISA
- Priority claim within 12 months of first filing

## Tips for Strong US Patents

1. **Draft claims first** — They define the invention. Write the specification to support them.
2. **Use means-plus-function carefully** — Requires corresponding structure in specification (35 USC 112(f)).
3. **Include fallback positions** — If broad independent claim fails, dependent claims should still offer meaningful protection.
4. **Describe alternatives** — Show the invention is not limited to one specific implementation.
5. **Define terms** — If using a term in a special way, define it explicitly in the specification.
6. **No new matter** — Never add new technical disclosure after the filing date.
